# Matthew Vaishnav

I'm an independent computational pathology researcher and research engineer based in Kitchener–Waterloo, Ontario. My research examines how image acquisition changes what machine-learning models learn about histopathological tissue. When a physical slide is scanned on different systems, the specimen remains the same even though its digital representation may change. I study that distinction using matched tissue observations.

I developed **Paired-Acquisition Neural Factorization (PA-NF)** as a custom computational pathology research framework built around this problem. The framework was designed as a complete experimental pipeline, with its neural models studied through paired-scanner interventions and comparisons that distinguish tissue information from acquisition-dependent variation. Its central question is whether these factors can be represented separately without sacrificing biologically useful information.

The work has been investigated on paired human histopathology scans and an independent multi-scanner canine dataset. My aim is not to assume that scanner invariance implies biological preservation, but to determine which properties are actually supported by the experimental observations.

My related work examines the reliability of quantitative tissue measurements under changes in computational context. I'm particularly interested in the scientific consequences of treating computational outputs as measurements of biology.

## Research and publications

**Paired-Acquisition Neural Factorization** is documented in the [public computational pathology research repository](https://github.com/matthewvaishnav/computational-pathology-research), with a [research manuscript](https://matthewvaishnav.github.io/computational-pathology-research/) and study-specific evidence from [paired human acquisitions](https://github.com/matthewvaishnav/paired-acquisition-factorization-scorpion) and an [independent canine study](https://github.com/matthewvaishnav/paired-acquisition-factorization-caninescc). The research remains experimental; the linked record describes the methods, comparison protocols, and the limits of their interpretation.

My [NucleoScope work](https://github.com/matthewvaishnav/nucleoscope) investigates how computational context influences quantitative measurements in digital histopathology.
